<h2 id="r-oportunidade">The opportunity</h2>

Project Aurora started from the chance to build a contemporary beat 'em up where Brazilian identity is part of the game itself: not just in the setting, but in the characters, regions, combat styles, progression and narrative. The challenge is turning that identity into systems, not just visual references.

<h2 id="r-meu-papel">My role</h2>

Within a three-person team, I am Product Lead and Game Design Lead: I own the vision, systems, scope and prioritization decisions. The two developers own engineering and implementation, and we decide feasibility, architecture and trade-offs together.

<h2 id="r-decisoes">Four product decisions</h2>

<h3 id="r-brasil-como-sistema">Brazil as a system, not just aesthetics</h3>

Characters with regional combat styles, regions with distinct characteristics, and progression and narrative tied to the universe: the differentiation is in the game's rules, not just the art.

<SeeFull to="brasil-como-sistema" label="See details" />

<h3 id="r-hub-diegetico">Menus become part of the world</h3>

Region selection, upgrades, NPCs and preparation concentrated in an underground hub that belongs to the universe, instead of separate menus. Trade-off: more art, content and implementation, in exchange for coherence across navigation, narrative and progression.

<SeeFull to="hub-diegetico" label="See details" />

<h3 id="r-ritmo-arcade">Depth without losing the arcade feel</h3>

Simple controls shared by every character; depth comes from timing, positioning, combos and combat styles. Risk to avoid: complex progression that turns the beat 'em up into a menu-heavy RPG.

<SeeFull to="ritmo-arcade" label="See details" />

<h3 id="r-validar-core">Validate the core before scaling content</h3>

A PoC confirmed movement, the combat base, base enemies, camera, HUD, mini-boss and the technical direction before investing in characters, regions, enemies and campaign. Trade-off: starting smaller to reduce technical and gameplay risk.

<h2 id="oportunidade">The Opportunity</h2>

Project Aurora started from the opportunity to build a contemporary beat 'em up where Brazilian identity is part of the game itself. Not just in the setting or the art, but in the characters, regions, combat styles, progression and narrative.

The challenge became turning that identity into systems, rather than relying on visual references alone.

<h2 id="meu-papel">My Role and Team</h2>

Project Aurora is being developed by a three-person team. I serve as Product Lead & Game Design Lead and own the main decisions around vision, systems, scope and prioritization.

My work is to turn the creative direction into a product the team can discuss, prioritize and build. That covers gameplay loop, characters, combat, progression, economy, campaign, hub and deciding what needs to be confirmed first.

The two developers own engineering decisions and implementation. We work together on feasibility discussions, architecture and technical trade-offs.

<h2 id="decisoes">Three Decisions That Shape the Experience</h2>

<h3 id="hub-diegetico">Turning Menus Into Part of the World</h3>

The game needed to connect region selection, upgrades, items, NPCs, narrative, preparation and progression. The simplest path would have been a set of independent menus. The decision was to centralize all of those functions in a diegetic hub, an underground space that belongs to the game's own world.

  titleA="Independent menus"

The trade-off is real: more art, content and implementation in exchange for greater coherence between navigation, narrative and progression. The full hub belongs to the product vision and will be built during the production phase.

<h3 id="ritmo-arcade">Adding Depth Without Losing the Arcade Feel</h3>

The product includes planned progression and character evolution systems. The design challenge was making sure those layers do not turn a beat 'em up into a menu-heavy RPG.

## Next Phase

With the technical core confirmed, the focus now is turning that foundation into a first complete slice of the experience.

What is in progress or defined for this phase:

- architecture consolidation
- combat refinement
- enemy systems
- first complete content
- HUD refinement
- progression systems begin
- hub systems begin

## Next Validations

Some product hypotheses still depend on playtesting to be answered. They are questions only a playable build resolves.

Once the first complete content is playable, it will be possible to evaluate how combat feels in longer sessions, the overall pacing, difficulty, objective clarity and how the first complete slice holds up as an experience.

As character, progression and hub systems come online, a second set of questions will follow: whether character variety reads clearly in combat, whether progression adds depth without pulling focus from the action, whether the balance between arcade rhythm and deeper systems lands in the right place, and whether the hub fulfills its role with clarity.
