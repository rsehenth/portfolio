## The Opportunity

Project Aurora started from the opportunity to build a contemporary beat 'em up where Brazilian identity is part of the game itself. Not just in the setting or the art, but in the characters, regions, combat styles, progression and narrative.

The challenge became turning that identity into systems, rather than relying on visual references alone.

## My Role and Team

Project Aurora is being developed by a three-person team. I serve as Product Lead & Game Design Lead and own the main decisions around vision, systems, scope and prioritization.

My work is to turn the creative direction into a product the team can discuss, prioritize and build. That covers gameplay loop, characters, combat, progression, economy, campaign, hub and deciding what needs to be confirmed first.

The two developers own engineering decisions and implementation. We work together on feasibility discussions, architecture and technical trade-offs.

## Three Decisions That Shape the Experience

### Turning Menus Into Part of the World

The game needed to connect region selection, upgrades, items, NPCs, narrative, preparation and progression. The simplest path would have been a set of independent menus. The decision was to centralize all of those functions in a diegetic hub, an underground space that belongs to the game's own world.

The product includes planned progression and character evolution systems. The design challenge was making sure those layers do not turn a beat 'em up into a menu-heavy RPG.

## Next Phase

With the technical core confirmed, the focus now is turning that foundation into a first complete slice of the experience.

What is in progress or defined for this phase:

- architecture consolidation
- combat refinement
- enemy systems
- first complete content
- HUD refinement
- progression systems begin
- hub systems begin

These items represent the Early Production path, not delivery commitments.

## What Still Needs Validation

Some product hypotheses still depend on playtesting to be answered. These are not identified problems. They are questions only a playable build can resolve.

Once the first complete content is playable, it will be possible to evaluate how combat feels in longer sessions, the overall pacing, difficulty, objective clarity and how the first complete slice holds up as an experience.

As character, progression and hub systems come online, a second set of questions will follow: whether character variety reads clearly in combat, whether progression adds depth without pulling focus from the action, whether the balance between arcade rhythm and deeper systems lands in the right place, and whether the hub fulfills its role with clarity.
