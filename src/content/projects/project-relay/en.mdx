## The problem

Running affiliate offers often means switching between marketplaces, spreadsheets, links, messages and communities. Selecting products, checking commercial information, preparing content and confirming each publication repeats work that is not connected in one flow.

Project Relay was designed to turn these steps into an organized operation while keeping commercial decisions with the operator.

## My role

I led the product end to end: defining the problem and value proposition, MVP scope, roadmap prioritization, operational experience, architecture decisions and implementation. The work involved translating commercial rules into flows, data and publication-safety criteria.

## The solution

Relay centralizes the product catalogue, affiliate profiles, collections, campaigns and message templates. The operator configures destinations and schedules; the automation engine processes distribution and records the outcome of every delivery.

<h2 id="problem">The operational problem</h2>

Affiliate marketing involves multiple marketplaces, accounts and communities. In a typical workflow, someone selects products, checks price and availability, retrieves links, writes messages, organizes offers and tracks publications in each channel.

When these activities happen in separate tools, information is repeated and it becomes difficult to answer what was published, to which destination, when and with which link. The problem was not simply automating messages; it was connecting the operation end to end.

## My role

I worked on the conception and construction of Project Relay, connecting product decisions to technical execution. I defined the value proposition, requirements, MVP scope, roadmap and operator experience, and also led data modelling, publication rules, architecture and implementation.

The criterion for each decision was to balance practical value, delivery feasibility and room to evolve. This made integrations, queues and failure states product decisions, since they directly affect the trust of someone running campaigns.

<h2 id="solution">The solution</h2>

Project Relay organizes the operation into six connected capabilities:

1. **Unified catalogue:** products from different sources with commercial information, categories, links and data origin.
2. **Affiliate profiles:** links tied to the correct profile to preserve attribution for every offer.
3. **Collections and campaigns:** reusable offers grouped into campaigns with destinations, frequency and schedule.
4. **Message templates:** content adaptable to titles, descriptions, prices, coupons and calls to action.
5. **Automated distribution:** persistent execution, scheduling, duplicate control and delivery history.
6. **Operational dashboard:** a view of products, campaigns, scheduled publications, deliveries and items needing attention.

<h2 id="decisions">Five decisions that shaped the product</h2>

<h3 id="integrations">1. Solve the operation before relying on APIs</h3>

The initial hypothesis included syncing products and links from marketplaces. The risk was structuring the product around capabilities that might not be available or authorized in each API. I therefore prioritized manual entry, imports and authorized integrations as independent paths.

**Trade-off:** the first experience does not depend on complete automation, while the operation remains useful as external connectors are evaluated.

<h3 id="catalogue-campaigns">2. Separate the catalogue from campaigns</h3>

The same product can appear in different campaigns with different channels, messages and schedules. The catalogue stores the reusable offer; a campaign defines distribution rules. This separation reduces duplication and allows information to be reused without losing context.

**Trade-off:** the initial model requires more care in exchange for future operational flexibility.

<h3 id="reliability">3. Prioritize safety before publication volume</h3>

An automation can send a message and fail before receiving external confirmation. Automatically repeating that send could create duplicates and harm the community experience. Relay distinguishes confirmed deliveries, recoverable failures and uncertain outcomes.

**Trade-off:** in ambiguous situations, the operation favors traceability and review before maximizing sends.

<h3 id="channel-independent-engine">4. Keep the automation engine independent from channels</h3>

Campaigns, scheduling and execution are not tied to a specific channel. The core logic is separated from distribution integrations so channels can be added or changed without rebuilding business rules.

**Trade-off:** a modular base takes an initial investment, but avoids both excessive coupling and premature microservice complexity.

<h3 id="lean-operations">5. Size infrastructure for the initial operation</h3>

The architecture uses Node.js, TypeScript and SQLite, with persistent tasks and preparation for a Linux VPS. This choice contains cost and maintenance while preserving failure recovery, data integrity and operational tracking.

**Trade-off:** the solution meets the current stage; higher volumes may require infrastructure review based on usage evidence.

The key boundary is between the operational core and external services. Marketplaces provide data when integration is feasible; channels receive publications through dedicated adapters. The catalogue and campaign continue working when a connector does not yet exist.

## Product and engineering challenges

### External platform dependencies

Marketplaces and channels each have their own permissions, limits and rules. The strategy was to center value in catalogue and distribution management, treating connectors as extensions evaluated one by one.

### Commercial-data integrity

Prices, availability and links change. The product must retain the source and update time, prevent publication with unsuitable data and retain the history needed for operational analysis.

### Automation reliability

Connectivity, channel limits and process failures are part of the environment. Automation should recover only safe tasks, prevent duplication and preserve evidence when an external result is uncertain.

### Simplicity for the operator

Queues, schedulers and execution states should not become interface complexity. The flow is organized around understandable actions: import, review, organize, schedule and track.

<h2 id="evolution">Evolution and impact assessment</h2>

Development was organized in stages: foundation and first publication, persistent and reliable automation, operational catalogue, campaigns, external connectors and continuous administrative experience. This sequence reduced premature dependencies and retained incremental value.

Impact will be assessed with real operational data, including campaign-preparation time, completed-delivery rate, manual intervention, distribution failures and operational cost. No quantitative gains are published until those measurements exist.

## Core learning

Automation is not about removing the operator from the operation. It is about deciding which tasks can run independently, which require validation and which evidence the system must preserve so that people retain control.
